Drainage-Crossing Detection from High-Resolution DEMs

Finding the culverts and bridges that elevation models leave out, so stream networks derived from LiDAR don't break at every road.

Problem

High-resolution digital elevation models (HRDEMs) from LiDAR capture road embankments but not the culverts and bridges that carry water underneath them. To a flow-routing algorithm every road looks like a dam, so derived stream networks are cut and diverted. Fixing this means knowing where the drainage crossings are, and mapping them by hand from aerial imagery doesn't scale.

Approach

  1. 1-m HRDEMLiDAR-derived
  2. Terrain derivativesTPI, openness, curvature
  3. Deep-learning modelclassify or detect
  4. ExplainGrad-CAM saliency
  5. Hydrographic mappingbreach the barrier

Two complementary tasks, each in its own paper:

  • Classification (Frontiers in AI, 2025): is there a crossing in this 100 × 100 m patch? EfficientNetV2, trained from scratch on combinations of the HRDEM and geomorphological layers derived from it, with explainable AI to check what the model attends to.
  • Detection (Environmental Modelling & Software, 2025): where exactly are the crossings? End-to-end pipelines comparing segmentation and object-detection architectures (U-Net, CM-UNet, Faster R-CNN, YOLOv5, Transformer-based models) on elevation-derived inputs.

Data

West Fork Big Blue Watershed, Nebraska: about 3,471 km² of mostly row-crop agriculture. The classification study uses 4,044 patches, balanced between crossing and non-crossing, with crossings digitized from aerial orthophotos. Each patch has five co-registered 1-m layers: HRDEM, curvature, positive openness, and TPI at two window sizes. A separate off-center test set checks that the model isn't just relying on crossings being centered.

The detection dataset is published: figshare, doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.25909453.

The best models improve on the earlier CNN baseline by 3.4–4.9 percentage points. More layers didn't help: stacking all five derivatives cut accuracy to 0.897, so feature selection mattered more than feature volume.

Contribution

Scientific: shows which terrain derivatives carry the crossing signal, and uses explainability to confirm the model learns the physical structure rather than artifacts.

Practical: a route to automatically conditioning LiDAR DEMs for hydrographic mapping at scale. Related work on bridging hydrographic gaps appeared in JAWRA (2024).

Does it transfer across regions?

CaGIS 2026 · Transferability of Earth foundation model embeddings for spatially implicit infrastructure mapping

Research question: Earth foundation-model embeddings are globally available, but global coverage of a representation does not guarantee geographic validity of the decision rule learned from it. Do drainage-crossing models trained on AlphaEarth embeddings retain predictive skill under zero-shot transfer to geographically distinct regions, relative to models trained on terrain covariates alone?

Models are trained on labeled crossings in Nebraska and evaluated, without retraining or local calibration, in Illinois, North Dakota and California.

Three input representations, DEM-derived terrain covariates, 64-dimensional AlphaEarth embeddings and their fusion, are each paired with two downstream learners (XGBoost and a CNN), giving six configurations.

Area under the precision–recall curve (PR-AUC):

  • Transfer is constrained by spatial heterogeneity. No configuration retains its in-domain skill across all three target regions, and the best-performing configuration differs by region.
  • The value of the embeddings is not uniform. Relative to terrain-only models, AlphaEarth inputs improved transfer in some model–region pairs and degraded it in others.

A natural next step is moving from prediction maps to applicability maps: estimating, before target-region labels are available, whether a region's predictor space is represented in the training data, for example with the area of applicability and dissimilarity index (Meyer & Pebesma, 2021).

Scope and next steps

  • The classification results come from a single watershed; the transfer study above tests generalization across states with one source region and three targets.
  • Classification works on patches. Turning detections into a conditioned DEM and a corrected stream network is the downstream step.
