Conservation Diagnostics for Deep Learning Groundwater Surrogates

A surrogate can reproduce hydraulic head to R² > 0.999 and still violate the physics it was trained on. This project builds the diagnostics to catch that.

Problem

Numerical groundwater models such as MODFLOW are accurate but slow, which limits how many pumping or recharge scenarios a planner can explore. Deep-learning surrogates promise near-instant predictions, and they are usually judged by fit statistics: RMSE, R². But a model that matches the simulator's head field cell by cell can still break the governing equation, misplace flow at the boundaries, or fail to conserve water. Accuracy screens don't see any of that.

Research question: does high predictive accuracy imply physical consistency in groundwater surrogates? If not, how do you measure the gap?

Approach

  1. Benchmark aquifersB1 uniform, B2 low-K lens
  2. MODFLOW-2005transient, 0–30 d, 33 snapshots
  3. Train surrogatesCNN · ConvLSTM · PINN
  4. Predict headabsolute metres
  5. Diagnoseaccuracy + physics

I trained three surrogate families on two MODFLOW-2005 benchmarks, then evaluated each one with a diagnostic package that measures accuracy and physics together:

  • Accuracy: MSE, MAE, RMSE, relative RMSE, R².
  • PDE residual: pointwise Boussinesq violation, reported as a ratio to MODFLOW's own residual under the same discrete operator. A ratio of 1 means the surrogate is as consistent as the solver that produced its training data.
  • Boundary compliance: head error and gradient-direction agreement at the Dirichlet boundaries.
  • Mass balance: storage change against boundary and well fluxes, as a percentage of the imposed extraction.
  • Zone and grid sensitivity: the same quantities split into well, lens and background zones, and re-evaluated on coarser stencils.

Data and models

Both benchmarks are transient, unconfined, single-layer aquifers on a 1000 × 1000 m domain discretized at 300 × 300, with a centered pumping well at −40,000 m³/d. B2 adds a 10:1 low-conductivity lens between the well and its main supply boundary, plus a regional gradient.

All six models would pass any conventional accuracy screen. The CNN and ConvLSTM carry residuals two orders of magnitude above the numerical baseline and fail to close mass balance. The physics-informed model is the only one with residuals at or below the numerical baseline, and it keeps mass-balance error within 1.5%.

A ratio below 1 doesn't mean the PINN is more correct than MODFLOW. MODFLOW is the reference: the ratio measures how much a head field violates the discrete Boussinesq operator. A smooth network output trained to minimize that residual can score below the solver while still differing from it, which is why the diagnostics report accuracy and physics side by side.

Contribution

Scientific: it shows, with numbers, that accuracy and physical consistency come apart for groundwater surrogates, and gives a reusable way to measure both.

Engineering: it ships a diagnostics package with unit-carrying outputs and a self-check test: reference data evaluated against itself must return zero head error and a residual ratio of exactly one. It has one data-root resolver, separate PINN trees per benchmark so configurations can't silently cross over, and demo notebooks that run the full pipeline on a CPU in minutes.

Scope and limitations

  • Both benchmarks are synthetic, single-layer and unconfined, which keeps the reference physics controlled. Field-scale, multi-layer systems are the next step.
  • The headline numbers are for the unconstrained CNN and ConvLSTM. Hard-boundary variants are in the repository so boundary enforcement can be tested independently of the PDE residual.
  • Model data (several GB) is distributed separately from the code.
